Why it exists

Since 1945, Argentina has had inflation of 10% or more in about 8 out of every 10 years.

  1. 1975The Rodrigazo: prices shoot up almost overnight.
  2. 1989Hyperinflation: nearly 200% in July alone, about 3,000% over the year.
  3. 2007INDEC is intervened. In 2013 the IMF censures Argentina over its statistics; the censure is lifted in 2016.
  4. 2023211% over the year.
  5. 20261.9% in September and 33.4% over twelve months, according to INDEC.

Sources: INDEC; Fundar (Argendata).

The official index still weighs prices by how households spent in 2004/05, more than 20 years ago.

And inflation hits hardest those who have least: the lower the income, the bigger the share spent on food.

We don't give opinions on inflation. We measure it, every day, and publish the data so anyone can check it.

  1. Collect

    Every morning, a program reads the online prices of 33 everyday products at Carrefour, Jumbo, ChangoMás and Día.

  2. Compare

    Each price is compared with the day before. Same shop, same product. Out of stock means skipped, never guessed.

  3. Weigh

    The changes are combined using INDEC's official weights, so what families spend more on counts more.
